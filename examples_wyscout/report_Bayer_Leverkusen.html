Across 10 teams (thesis validation), 8 to 10 matches were needed for passes and long-pass tendencies to be within half the gap between teams; shot rate and the share of sequences starting in the own half could not be told apart between teams reliably even with 15 matches. Families here are fitted on all available matches; with only a few matches the families themselves would be less stable. Recent form and tactical changes are not captured.

5. How to read this report

  • Families are slices of how this team builds up, fitted on its own matches: they describe tendencies, not fixed tactical plans. Names such as B1 are labels, not tactical categories.
  • Stability is the mean best-match Jaccard under bootstrap over matches (stable 0.75 or more, moderate 0.60-0.75, unstable below). When the structure check says 'slices of a continuum', treat the families as a convenient partition, not as separate behaviours.
  • Validation on held-out matches (10 teams): families are reproduced (shares change by at most 10 points) and how a sequence starts explains almost all of the predictability; score, home/away, minute and previous family added nothing (exploratory).
  • The families do not predict whether a sequence ends in a shot better than a one-variable rule based on where the sequence ends (families better in 0 of 10 teams, simple rule better in 5 of 10). Read them as a description of how the team builds up, not as a better shot predictor.
  • Limits: event data only, no player positions off the ball; the tactical meaning of the families has not been validated by coaches; shot rates per family are descriptive, not causal.
